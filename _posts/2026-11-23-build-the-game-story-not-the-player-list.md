Most people build a DFS lineup like they're filling out a grocery list. Best quarterback I can afford, check. Two running backs, check. Three receivers, check. Done.

Then they wonder why their lineup of "good players" finishes in the middle of the pack every single week.

## Points come in bunches

Football is the most connected sport in fantasy. When a quarterback throws for 350 yards, those yards went *somewhere*. His receivers had big days too. And if he's throwing that much, odds are the other team was scoring, which is why he kept throwing.

One thing going right causes three other things to go right. That's correlation, and it's the most powerful tool you have.

So stop building from a ranked list of players. Build from a game.

## Start with the story

Before I pick a single player, I ask: **what has to happen for this lineup to win?**

"My quarterback throws four touchdowns" is not a story. "This game turns into a shootout, both teams are over 30, and the total blows past what Vegas set" is a story. Once you've got the story, the players pick themselves.

The skeleton I use most in big tournaments looks like this:

1. **The quarterback**, from the game you think goes off.
2. **One or two of his pass catchers.** If he scores, they scored.
3. **A bring-back:** one player from the *other* team in that same game. If it's a shootout, somebody on the other side is getting points too.

Then fill in the rest of the roster from other spots on the slate.

## Things that work against each other

Some combinations quietly cancel each other out:

- **Your quarterback plus his own team's defense.** Your defense scores when the other offense struggles. That usually means a slow, low-scoring game, which is the opposite of what you need from your quarterback.
- **Two running backs from the same team.** They split carries. When one has a huge day, it's often because the other didn't get the ball.

None of these will kill you on their own. But you're paying full price for a ceiling that's fighting itself.

## The leverage is right next to the chalk

Here's where the story approach really pays off. Say everybody loves one team's top receiver this week. He's on 30% of rosters.

You like that game too. You think it's going to shoot out. Fine. But instead of the star receiver, take the *other* receiver or the tight end at 5% ownership. Same game, same quarterback, same story. A sixth of the crowd.

If the game goes off, you've got a piece of it that almost nobody else does.

## Tell a different story than everyone else

If you enter more than one lineup, don't make five versions of the same lineup. Write different stories:

- **Lineup 1:** "Game X turns into a shootout."
- **Lineup 2:** "The favorite blows them out, and their running back and defense eat."
- **Lineup 3:** "The chalk is right, plus one smart pivot."

If one of those stories comes true, you're in it. That beats being 90% sure about a lineup that's identical to ten thousand others.
