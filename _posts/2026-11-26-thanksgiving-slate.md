Happy Thanksgiving. Before the turkey, a receipt.

## Last night: wrong game, right build

I said Packers 32, Rams 18. The Rams won 26-13. That's not a near miss. That's a different sport.

But the build held up. I said fade Stafford, and the Rams threw 23 passes against 46 runs. I said captain a Rams running back, and Blake Corum ran 32 times for 184 yards and a touchdown. Most carries by anyone in the league this year. I had him catching dump-offs in garbage time. He caught two. He didn't need garbage time.

The rest of it: Kyren Williams added a short touchdown. Brandon McManus went 4 for 4 on field goals. The Rams defense picked off Jordan Love twice and forced a fumble. Christian Watson caught Green Bay's only touchdown, then left with a concussion in the third quarter. Josh Jacobs didn't play.

So: wrong about who wins, right about how it gets won. Right half the time is worth something. Last night it was worth a Corum captain.

## The numbers I'm working from

Everything below comes from my own projections for today's three games, next to Stokastic's DraftKings projections and ownership. Where the two disagree, I say so. Ownership is a projection, not a fact, until the contests lock.

## Bears at Lions (noon Central)

The total is 55.5, so on paper this is the shootout of the day. Part of me thinks it turns into a stalemate. Chicago's defense is legit, especially with Jeffery Simmons up front since the deadline. He has 5 sacks in 3 games as a Bear. Detroit is also without Aidan Hutchinson for the year.

**Chicago side.** I have Caleb Williams for 2 to 3 touchdown passes, about 25 rushing yards, maybe a rushing score. Call it 21.5 points with a ceiling near 30. My model is lower at 19.1. Stokastic is higher at 23.0 and has him around 31% owned at $5,800. His main target is Luther Burden III. Rome Odunze is good for 2 or 3 catches, and if he's the one who breaks free, something like 3 for 65 and a touchdown. D'Andre Swift is their guy: say 70 rushing yards, 20 receiving and maybe a score. Kyle Monangai gets the goal line work, so 45 yards and a possible touchdown. Colston Loveland could be the surprise. Stokastic has him 36% owned, which means he won't surprise many people.

**Detroit side.** Jared Goff, Jahmyr Gibbs, Amon-Ra St. Brown, Sam LaPorta, Jameson Williams and sometimes Isaac TeSlaa. Gibbs is $9,500 and projected around 56% owned by Stokastic. He's the playmaker, and everyone knows it. If you want to stack Goff with Gibbs and LaPorta or St. Brown and go all in, I'm fine with that.

**Defenses.** I wouldn't play either. If I had to, it's Chicago for the sack potential, holding Detroit to 16 or 17.

**My top three from this game:** Caleb Williams, Burden, Gibbs.

## Eagles at Cowboys (3:30 Central)

Philadelphia is in absolute shambles. Joe Milton III starts. Saquon Barkley is the only Eagle worth talking about. Milton is cheap at $4,600 if you need salary, and I'd put him at 12 to 14 points. Makai Lemon is the other cheap piece. DeVonta Smith is back, and he saw 12 targets last week, so he isn't a secret. Dallas Goedert is, somehow, still out there playing football. And yes, Dallas's defense has been bad. The Cowboys made the Titans look like a real team last week.

On the Dallas side it's CeeDee Lamb, George Pickens and Javonte Williams. Everyone wants the Dallas passing game. I think **Javonte Williams is the leverage**. He ran for 155 yards the last time these two played.

Here's where the slate gets weird. **Javonte plus the Cowboys defense** is my favorite correlated play. When the running back is eating, the clock runs, and a Milton-led offense is taking sacks. Stokastic has the Cowboys defense around 23% owned, so this isn't a secret, but pairing it with the Dallas back instead of the Dallas passers is. If you're running 20 lineups, give one of them to a cheap Philadelphia stack, Milton with Lemon, just in case.

## Chiefs at Bills (7:20 Central)

This should be a fantastic game. These two played one of the best games I've ever seen: the January 2022 divisional round, when Buffalo took the lead with 13 seconds left and Kansas City still tied it and won 42-36 in overtime. Tonight there's a real chance of cold rain, and the total has already dropped from 50.5 to 46.5.

**Buffalo.** Josh Allen is the most expensive quarterback on the slate at $7,000. He needs 35 points to pay off. It's possible, but I don't see it. James Cook always seems to reach his value. Dalton Kincaid is questionable. I really like Khalil Shakir, and I like him with Dawson Knox. That's the story where you don't just go to the top two options. You find the soft spots in Kansas City's defense. Knox is a big body who should get one-on-ones near the goal line. Shakir moves the chains on third and 8. DJ Moore is the 20- to 30-yard shot into the end zone if the coverage breaks.

**Kansas City.** Patrick Mahomes is $6,000 and now has Josh Downs, who I think matters. Kenneth Walker III is out, so the backfield belongs to Emmett Johnson. He's going to be one of the most popular backs on the slate. Do you eat the chalk? Probably, in 3 or 4 lineups. Then you spread the rest, because that's what wins tournaments.

I wouldn't play either defense, except maybe one for the weird chance. Football is football.

## Me vs. my model

Everything above is me talking. I also run a model every week. It projects every player, simulates the slate thousands of times, and builds my real lineups under rules I've graded all season. Below I checked my takes against it, plus Stokastic's ownership projections, so you can see where the gut and the math line up and where they don't. Ownership numbers are projections until lock.

**Where we agree**

- **Caleb Williams.** He's the model's most-used quarterback, in 6 of my 20 lineups. On points we're close but not identical: I said 21.5, the model says 19.1, and Stokastic says 23.0.
- **Gibbs is the best play in Bears-Lions.** The model projects him highest on the slate at 25.9, and he's in 9 of 20.
- **Javonte Williams is a core piece.** The model projects him at 18.0 and has him in 9 of 20.
- **Javonte with the Cowboys defense.** The model pairs them in 3 lineups.
- **Eat the Emmett Johnson chalk in a handful of lineups.** I said 3 or 4. The model has him in 5 of 20.
- **Josh Allen isn't worth $7,000.** The model projects him at 20.2 and uses him only twice.

**Where the model disagrees with me**

- **Javonte isn't leverage by ownership.** Stokastic has him around 41% owned, more than Lamb (38%) or Pickens (31%). I like him for the game script, but the crowd likes him too.
- **The Dallas passing game.** I'm fading Lamb and Pickens. The model loves them: Lamb is in 9 of 20 and Pickens in 8. That's our biggest disagreement on the slate, and it's the model's biggest disagreement with Stokastic too.
- **Chicago's pass catchers.** The model is more into Loveland (8 of 20), Odunze and Swift (7 each) than my lineup is.
- **Dawson Knox.** He's true leverage at about 4% owned, but the model projects him at only 4.5 points and uses him twice. My touchdown thesis is the whole bet there.
- **The Bears defense.** If forced, I'd take Chicago's defense. The model projects it at 5.6, behind the Cowboys at 7.8.

**How my lineup stacks up against the model's 20**

- **Projected points:** 116.7, right around the average of the model's lineups.
- **Ownership:** combined, about 199%. That's lower than 18 of the model's 20 lineups. It's a more contrarian build, which is the point.
- **It breaks two of the model's rules.** First, it plays the Cowboys defense against an Eagle in the same lineup, Makai Lemon. The model never lets a defense face my players. I'm doing it on purpose, because a Milton-led offense that leans on Lemon is exactly the game script where the Cowboys get sacks. Second, Caleb only has one teammate with him (Burden). The model wants a quarterback plus two pass catchers in most lineups.
- **The $800 I left on the table.** The model can go either way on this. My own showdown data says spending more salary has scored better, while outside research says spending less means fewer duplicate lineups. I'm taking the uniqueness tonight.
