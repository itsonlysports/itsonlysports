Packers at Rams, Wednesday night, 7 p.m. Central at SoFi. The night before Thanksgiving, so half of you will be watching this from a bar you haven't been to since high school. One catch: it's on Netflix. So before you commit to that bar, ask if they have Netflix. If they don't, you're watching on a phone propped against a pint glass, or you're asking your cousin for their password again. Enjoy it.

The books have the Rams favored by 2.5 with the total at 44.5. That's roughly Rams 23.5, Packers 21. I don't buy it.

## Why I'm taking Green Bay

The Rams are without Puka Nacua (knee surgery, likely done for the year) and Davante Adams (hamstring). Puka was soaking up somewhere around a quarter to a third of the targets. Take that away and who's left? Konata Mumpfield and Tutu Atwell are the top two receivers now, and the tight ends, Tyler Higbee and Colby Parkinson, are suddenly a big part of the plan. When your tight ends have a better touchdown chance than your wide receivers, something is not great.

The defense is finally healthy, Myles Garrett and Aaron Donald included. Fine. It's also an older group, and Green Bay has a lot of firepower. Indoors, on turf, no weather to hide behind, that firepower plays faster.

Green Bay is coming in hot after losing to Minnesota. Night game, holiday eve, a team with something to prove against a team with one healthy way to move the ball. I just don't see the Rams holding them off.

My call: **Packers 24, Rams 17** on the conservative side. **Packers 32, Rams 18** if it gets away from them.

## The Showdown build

Here's where it gets fun. If the Rams only have a running game, then the Rams running backs are the Rams passing game too. When LA falls behind, the ball goes on short dump-offs to the backfield. I'd put Blake Corum down for at least five catches, and Kyren Williams for about the same.

Before the lineups, a few rules I've learned the expensive way this season. These come from my own graded Showdowns, not from a guru:

- **The captain is the slate.** Write the football reason for your captain before you look at any optimizer. The week I picked a captain off a projection column with no story, more than 15,000 people captained the same guy and none of them reached the top 1%.
- **Low-owned captains have been my best edge.** When I gave a big chunk of my lineups to captains under about 5% owned, it worked five slates out of five. One of those captains was owned 1.4% and won the whole thing.
- **The field's favorite captain is usually a trap.** Five times out of six this season, the most-captained player barely showed up in the top 1%. The captain slot costs 1.5x the salary, and the crowd pays that tax every week.
- **Never go 6-0.** In 26 perfect Showdown lineups from Weeks 10 and 11, the split was 4-2 ten times, 3-3 nine times and 5-1 seven times. Zero were all one team. Five of those 26 had a captain priced at $5,000 or less.

One honest note: I don't have tonight's ownership projections, so everything below is my read of where the crowd goes, not a number. My guess is Jordan Love and Kyren Williams are the popular captains, because one is the quarterback I think wins and the other is the only healthy weapon LA has. That's why Corum is the purer leverage play of the two backs.

## Three builds, not one lineup

If you're running 20 entries, here's how I'd split them. Running five? Keep the same ratios: two, two and one.

**1. The Rams back gets the garbage-time catches (8 lineups).** Captain Corum in most of them, Kyren in a few. Then a 4-2 Green Bay onslaught in the flex: Love plus Christian Watson or Matthew Golden, with a cheaper pass catcher like Tucker Kraft. The story: Green Bay builds a lead, the Rams keep running and dumping it off, and the back piles up catches. If that happens, almost nobody else has this lineup.

**2. Green Bay through the air (8 lineups).** Captain Love, Watson or Golden, with two Packers pass catchers and one Rams running back as the bring-back. Don't put all eight on one captain. Whoever you think the crowd loves most gets two at most. The story is simple: I think Green Bay wins, so some of my lineups should look like it.

**3. The cheap captain (4 lineups).** Captain the Packers defense or Tucker Kraft, then spend the savings on Love and both top receivers. If the Rams can't throw, somebody is getting sacked and somebody is throwing a pick. A cheap captain also leaves room to fit the guys everyone else had to squeeze out.

**Across all of them:**

- **Fade Matthew Stafford.** I don't think the Rams throw the ball one bit beyond the short stuff. That's where his yardage comes from, and that's not a captain.
- **Josh Jacobs is doubtful.** If he somehow plays, he's leverage, because nobody will have him. I still don't think he makes much of an impact. If you're running multiple entries, put him in a few, and strictly only if he's active.
- **Skip Skyy Moore.** I know people will play Bo Melton. I'm not touching Moore at all.

If Green Bay wins big and a Rams back catches six balls, build one is the lineup nobody else has, and build two is right there with it. If I'm wrong about the game, I'm wrong. That's the deal.

Right half the time is worth something. Tonight's receipt goes on the [Receipts](/receipts/) page either way.
