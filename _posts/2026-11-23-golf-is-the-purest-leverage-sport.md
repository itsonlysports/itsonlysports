Here's a fact that should change how you play fantasy golf: **the best golfer on the planet, at his absolute peak, wins maybe 15 to 20% of the time.**

Think about that. The favorite, the guy on the cover, the one everybody's rostering, loses four out of five weeks. In what other sport does the best player lose that often?

That's why golf is my favorite sport for DFS. Nowhere else is the crowd more wrong, more often.

## Why the crowd gets golf wrong

People pick golfers the way they pick restaurants: by name and by whatever they saw last.

- **Name value.** The big names get rostered because they're the big names.
- **Last week.** Whoever won last Sunday gets piled on this week, even if this week's course is a terrible fit for his game.
- **TV time.** If he was on the broadcast a lot, he feels like a sure thing.

Meanwhile, the stuff that actually predicts a good week gets ignored: how well a golfer's game fits *this* course, how sharp his iron play is, and even which half of the field gets the better weather.

## The cut changes everything

In golf DFS, the cut is the whole ballgame. Miss it and your golfer plays two rounds instead of four, and you get no finishing-position points. He's basically a zero.

To win a big tournament you almost need all six of your golfers to make the cut. That's a big ask, and it's exactly why a cheap golfer has to have a real reason to be on your roster. "He's cheap" is not a reason. "His game fits this course and the field is weak this week" is.

## Where I find the edge

**1. Start with the betting odds.** Vegas puts real money behind its numbers. Turn a golfer's odds to win or finish top 5 into a percentage, and that's your starting point for how likely he is to have a huge week.

**2. Compare it to how many people own him.** This is where golf gets fun. You'll regularly find two golfers with about the same odds of a top 5, where one is on 25% of rosters and the other is on 8%. Same chance, a third of the crowd. Take the 8%.

**3. Check the weather.** Golfers tee off in morning and afternoon groups. If the wind is calm in the morning and howling in the afternoon, the morning group has a real advantage. Most people never look. It's free.

**4. Course fit over course history.** "He's played great here before" sounds smart, but four rounds is just noise. What matters is whether his strengths match what the course demands. Long hitters at long courses, great iron players where the greens are hard to hit.

## The traps

- **Last week's winner** at a course that doesn't suit him. Almost always overowned.
- **The "safe" golfer** who always makes the cut but never contends. That's a great pick for a cash game and a waste in a tournament. Tournaments are won by golfers who shoot 25 under, not 6 under.
- **Ignoring who's actually playing.** A golfer who won a weaker event against half a field isn't suddenly a threat against the best players in the world.

## The bottom line

Golf rewards the person who does the homework the crowd skips. The favorite loses most weeks. The cut is brutal. And the gap between what the odds say and what people roster is bigger here than in any other sport.

That gap is where you win.
